Directivity with and without the metasurface

The 6 × 5 split-ring metasurface 2 mm behind the antenna makes it more directive over most of the matched band: more of the power goes forward, which is what the metasurface was added for.

What is plotted

CST's “Directivity, Phi = 0, Max. Value”: the highest directivity in the φ = 0° plane at each frequency. Directivity is the gain the antenna would have with no losses. Realized gain, which also counts the FR-4 losses and the mismatch, is the next simulation.

Where the data comes from

Digitised from the two CST screenshots to about ±0.05 dB. The metasurface run has farfield samples every 0.1 GHz from 1 to 6 GHz, so the comparison stops at 6 GHz. Below 2 GHz neither antenna is matched.

Reading the 5–6 GHz gap

The largest difference, +7.3 dB at 5.9 GHz, is partly because the antenna alone points its beam out of this plane near 5.5 GHz. The fair headline is the peak, 3.7 → 8.8 dBi, and the 78 % of the band where the metasurface is higher.
